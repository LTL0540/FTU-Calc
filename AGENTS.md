## Product design and workflow principles

These principles guide UX decisions only; more specific clinical, safety,
architecture, and project instructions take precedence.

- Optimize the default workflow before exposing customization.
- Help users make clinical decisions, not operate the interface.
- Prefer safe, intelligent defaults; reduce, infer, merge, or automate before adding UI.
- Keep one clear next action where practical and use progressive disclosure for advanced controls.
- Preserve expert efficiency through direct manipulation and reversible actions.
- Use consistent patterns, terminology, spacing, hierarchy, and feedback across the app.
- Treat explanatory text as a fallback for unclear interaction; prefer clear labels and contextual hints.
- Support edge cases without letting them dominate the common path.
- Prefer the smallest change that meaningfully improves usability and preserve useful existing capability.
